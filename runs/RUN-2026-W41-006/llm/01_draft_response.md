## What changed this week

### 1. Commission convenes special AI panel meeting on loss-of-control incidents — EU — Relevance: Medium
- What happened: The European Commission held a special meeting of its Scientific Panel on artificial intelligence on 09 Oct 2026, attended by Henna Virkkunen, Executive Vice-President for Tech Sovereignty, Security and Democracy [W01]. The panel has been investigating "recent loss-of-control incidents" and worked with the AI Office on questions for the companies that developed the models involved [W01]. Virkkunen said Europe is "well equipped" to deal with rogue AI agents, according to Cryptopolitan citing Reuters [W02].
- Why it matters to us: The incidents concern autonomous agents like our pilots (P3), and the developers involved could include our US providers (P2) (Inference). Direct EU AI Act duties on us depend on our placing nothing on the EU market (Assumption P4).
- Action: No action — monitor.
- Confidence: Medium — official page, but it reports no recommendations, decisions or findings [W01].

### 2. UAE federal report cites agentic AI project and new AI authority — UAE (federal) — Relevance: Medium
- What happened: A WAM report dated 11 Oct 2026 says the federal Agentic AI Project aims to convert 50% of federal government sectors, services and operations to "autonomous, AI-driven models within two years" [W10]. It says the National Committee for Agentic AI approved its operating framework and introductory guide [W10]. It also says the UAE announced the Federal Authority for Artificial Intelligence and Data, to integrate national data, AI and digital government systems [W10].
- Why it matters to us: A new federal body could later set data or AI requirements for private organisations, but the report states no such powers (Inference). Our agent pilots (P3) sit in the same technology area (Inference).
- Action: Legal · find the primary instrument establishing the authority and check whether it applies to us · by 06 Nov 2026.
- Confidence: Medium — High-grade report updates the earlier Low-grade basis for the authority; announcement date and powers are not given [W10].

### 3. Dubai launches agentic AI accelerator for 33 government entities — UAE (Dubai) — Relevance: Low
- What happened: The "Agentic AI for Government Services Accelerator" starts with 33 Dubai Government entities assessing more than 300 public-facing use cases [W08]. Participants will work with technology companies certified under the Dubai AI Seal [W08]. Dubai Electronic Security Centre CEO Yousuf Hamad Al Shaibani said "Cybersecurity cannot be an afterthought in the adoption of AI," citing the Information Security Regulation in Dubai and the AI Security Policy [W08].
- Why it matters to us: This is a government programme, not a rule, so no duty on us follows (Inference). The cited Dubai frameworks may signal local security expectations for agentic AI (Inference).
- Action: No action — monitor.
- Confidence: Medium — single Medium-grade press source [W08].

### 4. UK ICO opens call for evidence on agentic AI — UK — Relevance: Low
- What happened: The UK Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) opened a call for evidence on 08 Oct 2026, closing 20 Nov 2026 [W06]. It asks how data protection law applies to agentic AI, to decide where to give more clarity before final guidance is published [W06]. The survey covers data security, transparency, accountability and automated decision-making, among other topics [W06].
- Why it matters to us: UK law applies to us only if we handle UK personal data, which the pack does not show (Inference). The topics overlap with our agent pilots (P3) (Inference).
- Action: Head of IT Risk · ask Legal whether UK data protection law applies and whether to respond · by 30 Oct 2026.
- Confidence: High — official ICO page; applicability to us is unconfirmed.
